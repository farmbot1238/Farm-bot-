// What is FARM8OT?

Redefining Pollination
With Sound & AI

FARM8OT is an intelligent agricultural system developed to address the decline in pollination efficiency in certain crops due to the reduction of bee populations and natural pollinators caused by intensive pesticide use and environmental factors. The project mimics the natural vibrations produced by bees during pollination using artificial intelligence, robotics, and drones. The system identifies plants with flowers ready for pollination through image analysis, then directs a drone equipped with targeted sound‑wave vibrations to stimulate pollen transfer within the flower. FARM8OT increases crop productivity, reduces the need for manual pollination, and offers an innovative solution supporting the future of smart agriculture and food sustainability.

// Key Innovations

Breakthrough Technologies
Behind Farm 8OT

🧠 Sipeed Maix RISC‑V Edge AI

The innovative core of the project lies in its custom‑designed computer vision system. A dedicated AI camera and processor were developed as an integrated standalone unit on a single board using the Sipeed Maix RISC‑V Edge controller, delivering an exceptional computing power of 1 TOPS. The key innovation is the ability to process and analyze data locally at the edge with extreme speed, eliminating the need for external cloud servers. This enables the robot to run advanced Ultralytics YOLOv8 algorithms efficiently, granting the system immediate autonomous capability to identify plant species and accurately determine plant age based solely on visual input.

🚀 Advanced All‑Terrain Suspension

The second innovation is the advanced mechanical design of the robot's chassis, specifically the six‑wheel suspension system. Inspired by NASA's Perseverance rover rocker‑bogie mechanism, this custom‑engineered suspension equally distributes weight across all six wheels and provides exceptional stability during motion. It ensures continuous ground contact even when traversing rough agricultural terrain and uneven slopes. This innovative engineering grants the robot superior kinematic flexibility and the ability to overcome obstacles smoothly, guaranteeing uninterrupted field operation while protecting the control boards and AI camera from excessive vibration.

🔊 Targeted Sound‑Wave Technology

This innovation represents an engineering leap in contactless mechanical energy transfer. It employs Acoustic Beamforming using a software‑controlled array of acoustic emitters to focus sound energy into a narrow, precisely directed beam rather than dispersing randomly. The scientific value lies in its ability to generate induced resonance vibrations at the target when the focused beam impacts, with frequencies tuned between 200 Hz and 350 Hz. This technique mimics the natural bio‑frequencies that stimulate mechanical interaction in target objects with high efficiency from a safe distance, serving as a revolutionary non‑contact alternative to traditional mechanical methods, completely protecting sensitive targets from physical tearing or damage.

// Project Versions

Evolution of FARM8OT

FARM8OT V1

The first prototype of the project, development started in early April 2025 and continued until mid‑May. This initial version was limited to fully manual movement control and plant type identification through color differentiation. It served as the foundation stone for the entire project.

FARM8OT V2

Development began in early July 2025 and lasted until mid‑June 2026. This second version was a breakthrough that transformed the project from a rudimentary stage to an advanced system. It introduced a six‑wheel robot with a suspension inspired by NASA's Perseverance rover, and employed advanced AI through a custom‑designed Sipeed Maix RISC‑V Edge AI processor and camera (1 TOPS). The robot became semi‑autonomous, capable of distinguishing plant types and growth stages using Ultralytics YOLOv8 algorithms. The key innovation was replacing ground‑based pollination with drone‑based targeted sound waves, marking a qualitative leap in the project.

FARM8OT V3

The third version will focus on developing the drone system and building a network connecting hundreds of drones to implement the next‑generation idea (which cannot be disclosed for intellectual property reasons). Development is expected to begin in early September.
